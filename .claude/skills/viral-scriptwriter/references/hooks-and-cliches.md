# Hooks

Good hooks make a promise the story keeps. Pick three of different types.

| Type | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction | Two facts that should not both be true | "He kept the lighthouse for thirty years and never once answered." |
| Vivid scene | Drop into a concrete moment | "Every night, the sea whispered his name." |
| Stakes first | Name what is at risk at once | "One boat. Four men. No light." |
| Open question | A question only this story answers | "Why did the sea go silent?" |
| Specific number | One exact, surprising detail (written as words) | "Forty seven keepers. One survived the winter." |

Test: could this line open a thousand other reels? Then it is generic. Rewrite with a detail only this story has.

# Phrases to remove (the reviewer flags these)

"in a world where", "little did he/she/they know", "delve", "tapestry", "unleash", "game-changer", "buckle up",
"let that sink in", "you won't believe", "what happened next", "shocked everyone", "changed everything forever",
"the rest is history", "mind-blowing", "dive into", "in today's", "at the end of the day".

Also avoid: rhetorical "But here's the thing", "And then... nothing.", triplets used as a tic ("fast, bold, unstoppable"),
and any moral spelled out after the story has already shown it.

# Satisfying ending

End on an image or an action that pays off the hook (the hook line returns changed). Do not summarise. Do not ask for engagement.
